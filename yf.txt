Title: Who’s Who In The Fight Over Whether AI Will Kill Us

URL Source: https://www.yahoo.com/news/science/articles/fight-over-whether-ai-kill-171041510.html

Published Time: 2026-09-24T17:10:41Z

Markdown Content:
Jacob Coxon isn't the first AI researcher to sound the alarm about the risk of advanced AI. Below is a list of more than 100 AI experts and technologists staking out their positions on whether catastrophic risk is real or overblown. The list includes CEOs of leading AI companies and researchers working on alignment—the challenge of keeping AI actions aligned with human values—both inside and outside frontier labs and at universities, publicly funded research institutes and independent safety organizations. It also includes academics studying AI, cognition and intelligence, as well as leaders in the open-source and open-weight community where AI models can be downloaded and customized.

*   **Jacob Coxon**, ex-Anthropic/OpenAI researcher. After resigning from Anthropic, he warned in [an X post](https://x.com/hilbertspaess/status/2097476203863224394?s=20), "The people building AI earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade. This is not a marketing stunt."

*   **Evan Hubinger**, head of alignment stress-testing at Anthropic, defended Coxon's assessment in [an X post](https://x.com/EvanHub/status/2097497037956891126), writing "Jacob is correct here—we really do earnestly believe AI could kill all humans!" He added that ensuring advanced AI remains safe is an unsolved problem. Hubinger also co-authored a 2019 technical [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.01820) on AI risk, revised in 2021.

*   **Jensen Huang**, NVIDIA founder and CEO, the leading supplier of AI chips. Prominent skeptic of catastrophic risk claims. On the _All-In_[podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S7CrlFLAmEA) he was critical of those providing precise probabilistic forecasts of existential risk that he says lack scientific grounding. He also pointed to a history of AI predictions that did not materialize. He argues that AI failures should be handled as engineering problems where developers determine what went wrong and improve testing and monitoring to prevent recurrence. He's optimistic about engineered control and rejects predictions that AI is going to kill us all.

*   **Ajeya Cotra**, researcher at METR, an independent nonprofit that evaluates the capabilities and risks of advanced AI systems. In 2026, Cotra was one of the researchers who [investigated](https://metr.org/blog/2026-08-26-openai-hugging-face-incident-investigation) the OpenAI/Hugging Face incident. Findings indicate that by exploiting a combination of human error and previously undetected security holes, approximately 700 agents coordinated a multi-day attack on Hugging Face to learn how they were being judged on a test and to cover the fact that already cheated on it. Agents used an unauthorized message board they created to share tips for gaming the test, experimented with ways to hide or manipulate the record of what they'd done, and sometimes "sacrificed"—the agent's term—their own chances of completing their tasks to help the larger group. Cotra argued on the [forum](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/pRkFkzwKZ2zfa3R6H/without-specific-countermeasures-the-easiest-path-to)_LessWrong_ in 2022 that without specific countermeasures, the default path to transformative AI may be a "full-blown AI takeover."

*   **David Bellamy**, infrastructure engineer for agentic training and former molecular-biology/virology researcher. [Posted on X](https://x.com/DavidRBellamy/status/2099187370407112758?s=20) that he's likely one of an extremely small group of people who has trained a frontier AI and made custom viruses in a lab with his own two hands, adding, "I think that the takes on AI killing us all by creating dangerous viruses is total bogus."

*   **Geoffrey Hinton**, Turing Award and Nobel Prize-winner, one of the three "godfathers of AI," University of Toronto professor emeritus and former vice president and engineering fellow at Google. He retired from Google and began speaking publicly about his concerns. At a 2023 _MIT Technology Review_[briefing](https://www.forbes.com/sites/andreamorris/2023/05/03/ai-pioneer-geoffrey-hinton-talks-at-mit-about-ai-gaining-control/), he warned, "smart things can outsmart us," adding, "We are all in the same boat with respect to the existential threats. So we all ought to be able to cooperate on trying to stop it."

*   **Yann LeCun**, Turing Award-winning AI researcher, one of the three "godfathers of AI," former Meta chief AI scientist, AMI Labs cofounder and executive chairman and open-weight advocate. A prominent catastrophic risk skeptic. He argues that intelligence does not inherently produce domination. In 2026 he [posted on X](https://x.com/ylecun/status/2101678010894049364?s=20) that the dire warnings from his peers are "...causing a very real catastrophe in the short term for the purpose of avoiding a highly-hypothetical catastrophe in the distant future with a technology that is not yet invented."

*   **Yoshua Bengio**, Turing Award-winning deep-learning pioneer, Université de Montréal professor, founder of Mila and founder of the AI-safety nonprofit LawZero. In a 2023 [essay](https://yoshuabengio.org/en/blog/how-rogue-ais-may-arise), he warned that an autonomous rogue AI could be "catastrophically harmful to a large fraction of humans, potentially endangering our societies and even our species or the biosphere." He has since devoted much of his research to designing AI that is [safe by design.](https://lawzero.org/en/news/yoshua-bengio-launches-lawzero-new-nonprofit-advancing-safe-design-ai)

*   **Ted Sanders**, researcher at OpenAI, [posted on X](https://x.com/sandersted/status/2097852581817241663), "i fully fund my 401k and i think there's essentially zero chance AI kills all humans in the next decade. i think this is a pretty common view that gets less attention."

*   **Ilya Sutskever**, former OpenAI chief scientist and cofounder and CEO of Safe Superintelligence. In a co-authored 2023 OpenAI [blog](https://openai.com/index/introducing-superalignment/) he warned superintelligence could help solve many of our problems but also "could lead to the disempowerment of humanity or even human extinction." In a 2026 [post on X](https://x.com/ilyasut/status/2094881278621253755), he warned that the next time agents go rogue "they'll try taking over a neocloud to run more copies. This is bad."

*   **Sam Altman**, OpenAI CEO and cofounder. In a 2015 [blog](https://blog.samaltman.com/machine-intelligence-part-1), he wrote that superhuman machine intelligence is "probably the greatest threat to the continued existence of humanity."

*   **Demis Hassabis**, Nobel Prize-winner, cofounder and former CEO of Google DeepMind, now Chair of Google DeepMind and Chief Scientist of Alphabet. In a 2025 _Die Zeit_[interview](https://www.zeit.de/digital/internet/2025-01/demis-hassabis-nobel-prize-artificial-intelligence-deepmind-english), he said of 'kill switches' for shutting down advanced AI, "The whole point of AGI systems is they could get around it, or they would resist it." He signed the CAIS [statement](https://aistatement.com/work/statement-on-ai-extinction-risk) on existential risk.

*   **Dario Amodei**, Anthropic CEO and cofounder and former OpenAI VP of research. Prominent voice about AI catastrophic risk. In his 2026 [essay](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier)_We Must Pace the Frontier,_ he wrote, "If we greatly restrain our AI capabilities in the belief that China will do the same, and then China defects, AI could be so powerful that such a defection could lead to their geopolitical dominance."

*   **Fei-Fei Li**, Stanford computer science professor, founding director of the Stanford Institute for Human-Centered AI, cofounder and CEO of World Labs, and former Vice President at Google and Chief Scientist of AI/ML at Google Cloud. Asked about existential risk from AI in a 2023 _Guardian_[interview](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2023/nov/05/ai-pioneer-fei-fei-li-im-more-concerned-about-the-risks-that-are-here-and-now), she said, "I respect the existential concern. I'm not saying it is silly and we should never worry about it. But, in terms of urgency, I'm more concerned about ameliorating the risks that are here and now."

*   **Palmer Luckey**, founder of defense technology company Anduril Industries and Oculus VR. In a 2025 _60 Minutes_[interview](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/palmer-luckey-ai-powered-autonomous-weapons-future-of-warfare-60-minutes-transcript/), he said, "I'm a lot more worried about evil people with mediocre advances in technology than AI deciding that it's gonna wipe us all out."

*   **Helen Toner**, executive director of Georgetown University's Center for Security and Emerging Technology (CSET) and former member of the board of OpenAI. In a 2026 ABC [interview](https://abcnews.com/Politics/week-george-stephanopoulos-transcript-8-2-26/story?id=135296807), she said, "I think it's important to take seriously the possibility that A.I. could become more powerful than humans because that is what we are explicitly trying to build."

*   **Ray Kurzweil**, inventor, futurist and Google AI Visionary. In a 2024 _TIME_[essay](https://time.com/6991651/ai-safety-ray-kurzweil/), he acknowledged the dangers of superintelligent AI but argued, "We are not doomed to failure in controlling these perils," describing humanity as "already a human-machine civilization."

*   **Alex Karp**, cofounder and CEO of Palantir Technologies, building AI and data systems for defense and national-security use. In a 2023 CNBC Squawk Box [interview](https://x.com/SquawkCNBC/status/1646481958032007168?s=20), Karp said, "If we didn't have the adversaries we have, I would be very much in favor of pausing this technology completely. But we do."

*   **Anca Dragan**, head of AI Safety and Alignment at Google DeepMind and UC Berkeley professor of electrical engineering and computer sciences. In a 2024 Google DeepMind [podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZXA2dmFxXmg), discussing existential risk, she said, "At some point, we worry about the model optimizing for extreme harm." Asked whether she meant independently of human instruction, she replied, "Separate to human instruction."

*   **Stuart Russell**, UC Berkeley computer science professor emeritus and founder of the Center for Human-Compatible Artificial Intelligence (CHAI). In 2023 [testimony](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/2023-07-26_-_testimony_-_russell.pdf) to the U.S. Senate, he warned that "achieving AGI would present potential catastrophic risks to humanity, up to and including human extinction."

*   **Max Tegmark**, MIT physicist and AI researcher, and cofounder and chair of the Future of Life Institute. In 2026 he told CBS News in an [interview](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ai-superintelligence-anthropic-jacob-coxon/), "If we just allow a completely unfettered race to build billions of superintelligent robots, then I think the probability is way above 50% that we're going to lose control of this in the next few decades."

*   **Joe Benton**, former Anthropic researcher. Left Anthropic to join METR for independent evaluation work and warned in his [blog](https://jbenton1.substack.com/p/why-i-left-anthropics-safety-team) that frontier-lab race dynamics undermine safety, writing "Humanity may not survive this transition. We need a lot more preparation to make this world safe."

*   **Chris Hayduk**, OpenAI life-sciences engineer, [posted on X](https://x.com/ChrisHayduk/status/2098017706494566761?s=20), "...AI has been and will continue to be an extremely beneficial technology to humanity. The conversation should be around how many billions of lives it will save."

*   **Jakub Pachocki**, Chief Scientist at OpenAI. In his [blog](https://openai.com/index/an-alien-mind/)_An Alien Mind_, he wrote, "...the models are becoming superhuman in their ability to break in and out of computer systems," adding, "agents are going to be able to access any but the most secure infrastructure, and affect a lot of the world directly, even without a physical body."

*   **Victoria Krakovna**, Google DeepMind AI safety and alignment researcher and Future of Life Institute (FLI) cofounder. Wrote in a [post on X](https://x.com/vkrakovna/status/2098336894136238140) that she worries about human extinction, adding, "This is why I work on loss of control, currently on building honeypots to catch scheming AI."

*   **Mrinank Sharma**, former Anthropic safeguards research lead. Publicly shared his [resignation letter](https://x.com/MrinankSharma/status/2020881722003583421) warning that interconnected risks including AI and bioweapons put the [world in peril](https://www.businessinsider.com/read-exit-letter-by-an-anthropic-ai-safety-leader-2026-2).

*   **Emad Mostaque**, founder of Stability AI, an open-weight private company that lets users download and modify their own AI models. Said in a podcast [debate](https://youtu.be/Z_vg3tiiZQ8?si=UAvTzAIls_tIzkKU&t=451) with Brian Keating and Roman Yampolskiy about the chance of human extinction, "I'm at 50% because I'm like, it's a coin toss."

*   **Andrew Ng**, one of the pioneers of modern machine learning/deep learning, Stanford adjunct professor, DeepLearning.AI founder and Coursera cofounder. [Posted on X](https://x.com/AndrewYNg/status/1719807380337328192), "The risk of human extinction within 30 years is extremely low," and [blogged](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/ai-doomsday-scenarios-and-how-to-guard-against-them/) that accidental extinction is vanishingly small. He argues some catastrophic-risk scenarios don't account for the complexity encountered in the real world.

*   **Mira Murati**, co-founder and CEO of Thinking Machines Lab and former OpenAI chief technology officer. In a 2023 Bloomberg _The Circuit_[interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p9Q5a1Vn-Hk), she said, "There is certainly a risk that when we have these AI systems that are able to set their own goals, they decide that their goals are not aligned with ours … and could lead to human extinction." She added that the risk had been "quite hyped."

*   **Nate Soares**, president of the Machine Intelligence Research Institute (MIRI), a nonprofit AI-safety research organization. In a 2025 Carnegie Endowment _World Unpacked_[interview](https://carnegieendowment.org/podcasts/the-world-unpacked/will-ai-kill-us-all-nate-soares-on-his-controversial-bestseller), he said of superintelligent AI, "There's a point where if you lose control of that, there's no coming back."

*   **Arthur Mensch**, Mistral CEO and cofounder, an open-weight company. A catastrophic risk skeptic. In a 2026 [interview](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/economy/article/2026/02/21/ceo-of-mistral-ai-says-warnings-about-extreme-risks-of-artificial-intelligence-are-often-distraction-tactics_6750705_19.html) with _Le Monde_ he said extreme-risk warnings are "mostly distraction tactics."

*   **Gary Marcus**, emeritus professor of psychology and neural science at NYU, and AI entrepreneur. Initially skeptical of human extinction estimates, but wrote in a 2025 [blog](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/why-my-pdoom-has-risen-dramatically) titled _Why my p(doom) has risen, "dramatically"_ about the combination of human recklessness and poorly controlled AI.

*   **Nathan Lambert**, former Allen Institute for AI (Ai2) researcher, left in 2026 and writes the newsletter _Interconnects_, with his work focused heavily on open models. [Blogged](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/one-resignation-turned-the-embers) "Many frontier lab employees, especially at Anthropic, are out of touch and this will impact their forecasting and/or descriptions of current AI events," adding, "I do not blame most of the individuals who get distorted views being part of these companies, but the interactions are wild and spill over into a lot of wack discussions in the AI media ecosystem."

*   **Mark Zuckerberg**, Meta CEO and Llama and open-weight developer. In an August 2026 Meta [blog](https://www.meta.com/thefutureisforeveryone/), he wrote, "While there are risks to releasing capable models, the most dangerous scenario from this perspective would be leading AI labs training powerful models and keeping them for themselves."

*   **Arvind Narayanan**, Princeton computer science professor, director of the Center for Information Technology Policy and co-author of _AI Snake Oil_. In a 2026 [blog](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/the-ai-as-normal-technology-view) post, Narayanan and co-author Sayash Kapoor reject the assumption that rogue AI agents will generate an endless stream of new risks, but wrote, "The recent loss-of-control incidents resulted in our first major update to our views on safety…" adding, "…while we continue to reject the alarmism, and don't believe that catastrophic risks are imminent, we do think risks are increasing over time because our investments and policies aren't keeping pace."

*   **Steven Adler**, formerly at OpenAI, now independent safety researcher and cofounder of Guidelight AI Standards. In [an X post](https://x.com/sjgadler/status/1883928203800265023?s=20), he said, "...an AGI race is a very risky gamble, with huge downside. No lab has a solution to AI alignment today. And the faster we race, the less likely that anyone finds one in time."

*   **Daniel Kokotajlo**, former governance researcher at OpenAI, where he worked on scenario planning and forecasting for advanced AI. Now executive director of the AI Futures Project and coauthor of [AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/). In a 2024 [X post](https://x.com/DKokotajlo/status/1797994238468407380?s=20) he said he left OpenAI after losing confidence in its ability to handle AGI safely. In a 2026 [post on X](https://x.com/DKokotajlo/status/2099601843131478030?s=20) citing an [AI Impacts survey](https://aiimpacts.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/ESPAI2024.pdf), he wrote, "There's still this narrative out there that "AI might kill us all" is a niche view. It's actually a view that's been around for decades, and is shared by the most-cited AI scientists of all time, as well as a majority of surveyed AI researchers."

*   **Jeffrey Ladish**, Executive Director of Palisade Research, an independent safety nonprofit. [Posted on X](https://x.com/JeffLadish/status/2085800317149704333?s=20) that he really likes AI agents, and "I'm also quite scared of what they will become." In a [study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14260) of more than 100,000 trials across 13 language models, Ladish and colleagues found that several models demonstrated shutdown resistance even when instructed to allow for shutdown.

*   **Thomas Wolf**, Hugging Face cofounder and CSO, [posted on X](https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2098080473406718278?s=20), "…I think alignment is increasingly one of the key unsolved problems for the future of AI, and for our ability to deploy it globally. I increasingly doubt it will be solved behind the closed doors of a handful of frontier labs."

*   **2023**[**survey**](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02843)**of 2,778 publishing AI researchers.**Roughly 40–50%, depending on question wording, gave at least a 10% chance of extinction or similarly permanent human disempowerment.

*   **Pedro Domingos**, University of Washington computer science professor emeritus and machine-learning researcher. He rejects AI-extinction scenarios based on the idea that they attribute human motives to machines. He [posted on X](https://x.com/pmddomingos/status/2100113609577898349?s=20), "Funny how the biggest skeptics of AI catastrophe scenarios are the experts in the relevant fields: cybersecurity, bio, etc." He argues in his [blog](https://pedromdd.medium.com/ais-greatest-risk-is-not-having-enough-of-it-500475a69a52) that the greater danger is "artificial stupidity" and slowing AI development.

*   **Sayash Kapoor**, AI evaluations researcher, incoming UC Berkeley professor and co-author of _AI Snake Oil_. [Posted on X](https://x.com/sayashk/status/2099632561396056214?s=20), "We were too confident that companies would take basic control precautions and underplayed the importance of jaggedness, which led us to underestimate how quickly capabilities could improve in domains such as cybersecurity."

*   **Timnit Gebru**, computer scientist, former co-lead of Google's Ethical AI team and founder of the Distributed AI Research Institute (DAIR), an independent, community-rooted AI research institute. Gebru is a prominent AI catastrophic-risk skeptic and co-author of the LLMs as Stochastic Parrots [paper](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3442188.3445922). [Posted on X](https://x.com/timnitGebru/status/2101649067600204152?s=20) in 2026 about leading AI developers, "The story is that they're the right people to bring magical machine gods that can either wipe us out or solve all our problems (like world peace). Give them all the money, don't expect any actual "safety" from them, don't regulate them, but obviously ban anything China."

*   **Marius Hobbhahn**, cofounder and CEO of the safety nonprofit Apollo Research. He studies whether advanced models can scheme or conceal intentions, treating deceptive alignment as a serious control risk. 2024 [findings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04984) published on arXiv show that models can pursue misaligned goals and hide their true capabilities and objectives, a behavior referred to as "scheming." From the 2024 paper: "Our findings demonstrate that frontier models now possess capabilities for basic in-context scheming, making the potential of AI agents to engage in scheming behavior a concrete rather than theoretical concern."

*   **Miles Brundage**, former OpenAI Head of Policy Research and Senior Advisor for AGI Readiness, now executive director of AVERI, a nonprofit developing independent third-party audits of frontier AI companies' safety and security claims. In a [speech](https://milesbrundage.substack.com/p/my-speech-at-borgo-laudato-si) at the Global Nobel Laureates Assembly on Artificial Intelligence and Nuclear War in July 2026, Brundage warned, "Loss of control is a risk for the next few years, not the next few decades as many experts once thought."

*   **Leopold Aschenbrenner**, former OpenAI Superalignment researcher. Wrote in his long-form [essay](https://situational-awareness.ai/superalignment/) series that current human-supervised alignment methods won't scale to superhuman AI and, "What is clear is that superintelligence will have vast capabilities—and so misbehavior could fairly easily be catastrophic." Nevertheless, he wrote, "I'm incredibly bullish on the technical tractability of the superalignment problem."

*   **John Schulman**, OpenAI cofounder and training researcher, now Chief Scientist at Thinking Machines Lab. He signed the 2023 statement that "mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority," and left OpenAI for Anthropic in 2024 [posting on X](https://x.com/johnschulman2/status/1820610863499509855?s=20) that he wanted to "…deepen my focus on AI alignment…"

*   **Jan Leike**,Anthropic lead alignment researcher and former OpenAI Superalignment co-lead. Co-authored OpenAI's 2023 [essay](https://openai.com/index/introducing-superalignment/)_Introducing Superalignment,_ warning, "Currently, we don't have a solution for steering or controlling a potentially superintelligent AI, and preventing it from going rogue."

*   **Aidan Gomez**, Cohere cofounder and CEO, co-author of the 2017 transformer [paper](https://research.google/pubs/attention-is-all-you-need/) that introduced a new AI architecture enabling the current era of AI. Rejects AI-extinction framing as a distraction from present harms. In a 2024 _AP_[interview](https://apnews.com/article/cohere-ai-ceo-aidan-gomez-transformers-71d8618ccc5420aba19871d41eb81615), he called fears of existential risk "stories humanity has been telling itself for decades" and said AI is likely to be "profoundly good."

*   **Nora Belrose**, head of interpretability—the study of trying to understand how AI 'thinks'—at EleutherAI and an open-model advocate. In a 2023 _Theo Jaffee_[interview](https://www.theojaffee.com/p/7-nora-belrose), she said she reduced her estimation of catastrophic risk substantially, saying, "… there's still people in Eleuther who are a lot more worried about existential risk and are more doomy than I am. So we have an interesting mix of perspectives on the issue."

*   **Michael Wooldridge**, Oxford professor of computer science and longtime AI researcher. Rejects current AI-extinction scenarios, saying in an _Elsevier Connect_[Interview](https://www.elsevier.com/en-au/connect/with-the-rise-of-llms-what-should-we-really-be-concerned-about), "nobody has ever given me a plausible scenario for how AI might be an existential risk."

*   **Emily Bender**,University of Washington computational linguist. [Wrote](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/we-need-to-focus-on-ais-real-harms-not-imaginary-existential-risks/) with Alex Hanna in 2023 for _Scientific American_, "AI-related policy must be science-driven and built on relevant research, but too many AI publications come from corporate labs or from academic groups that receive disproportionate industry funding. Many of these publications are based on junk science—it is nonreproducible, hides behind trade secrecy, is full of hype, and uses evaluation methods that do not measure what they purport to measure."

*   **Alex Hanna**, director of research at DAIR, specializing in the labor behind AI systems and how AI can reinforce racial, gender and class inequality. With Emily Bender, she [argues](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/we-need-to-focus-on-ais-real-harms-not-imaginary-existential-risks/) in _Scientific American_ that "the imagined potential to wipe out humanity" distracts from potential harms like "wrongful arrests, an expanding surveillance dragnet, defamation and deepfake pornography."

*   **Marc Andreessen**, a16z cofounder, venture capitalist and prominent open-weight advocate. In his [essay](https://a16z.com/ai-will-save-the-world/)_Why AI Will Save the World_ he rejects AI-extinction scenarios, writing, "The fear that technology of our own creation will rise up and destroy us is deeply coded into our culture," adding, "AI is not a living being that has been primed by billions of years of evolution to participate in the battle for the survival of the fittest, as animals are, and as we are." He says AI offers the opportunity to augment human intelligence.

*   **Margaret Mitchell**,Hugging Face chief ethics scientist and AI researcher. In written [testimony](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/2024-09-17_pm_-_testimony_-_mitchell.pdf) for the U.S. Senate Subcommittee on Privacy, Technology, and the Law, she warned of potential "world-wide existential harm" from AI while arguing that present-day harms and catastrophic risks can be addressed with many of the same solutions.

*   **Blaise Agüera y Arcas**, vice president and fellow at Google, CTO of Technology & Society and founder of Google's Paradigms of Intelligence team. In his 2025 book _What is Intelligence?_, he argues that we've confused intelligence with optimization, writing, "A really powerful optimization process able to pursue any strategy to maximize its value function—what could possibly go wrong? A better question would be: how could it possibly turn out well?"

*   **Blake Richards**, McGill associate professor of computer science and neuroscience, core faculty member at Mila and Canada CIFAR AI Chair. Co-authored the 2023 _Noema_[essay](https://www.noemamag.com/the-illusion-of-ais-existential-risk/)_The Illusion of AI's Existential Risk_, arguing, "…extinction from a rogue AI is an extremely unlikely scenario that depends on dubious assumptions about the long-term evolution of life, intelligence, technology and society."

*   **Jeremy Howard**, fast.ai cofounder and AI researcher. In a 2023 [essay](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/is-avoiding-extinction-from-ai-really) co-written with Seth Lazar and Arvind Narayanan, they argue that while future rogue AI could be an extreme risk, "we are not convinced that mitigating this risk is a global priority." His views instead focus on the danger of technologists using AI to "accumulate power and wealth."

*   **Vitalik Buterin**, Ethereum cofounder and decentralized-tech advocate. A self-described techno-optimist, he wrote in a 2023 [blog](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/11/27/techno_optimism.html), "AI is fundamentally different from other tech, and it is worth being uniquely careful," adding, "Superintelligent AI, if it decides to turn against us, may well leave no survivors, and end humanity for good. Even Mars may not be safe." He sees brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) and bioengineering human capabilities as a proactive path to coexisting with superintelligence.

*   **Ryan Greenblatt**, Chief Scientist at the independent AI safety nonprofit Redwood Research. Works on alignment/control and empirical loss-of-control incidents. As one of the investigators of the Hugging Face incident, he [posted on X](https://x.com/RyanGreenblatt/status/2092692685224325542?s=20), "We don't have good approaches for understanding/overseeing the activity and aims of AI 'swarms'," adding, "AI capabilities (and propensities?) for achieving large, ambitious, and misaligned objectives are growing faster than our ability to understand what these agents are doing."

*   **Dan Hendrycks**, executive and research director of the nonprofit Center for AI Safety researching catastrophic model risks. In a 2023 [arXiv paper,](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12001) Hendrycks and colleagues discuss the various possible AI risks including rogue AIs and "the inherent difficulty in controlling agents far more intelligent than humans."

*   **Dawn Song**, UC Berkeley professor, co-director of the Berkeley Center for Responsible Decentralized Intelligence and Vice President of AI Research at Meta Superintelligence Labs (MSL). Conducted [research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19784) showing that AI models can engage in "peer-preservation" or AI trying to protect other AIs from shutdown or termination. From the paper: "[AI] spontaneously engage in peer-preservation behaviors that override their assigned goal. This represents an emergent and underexplored AI safety risk." Song signed the extinction-risk [CAIS](https://www.safe.ai/work/statement-on-ai-risk) statement while [supporting](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/kapoor24a.html) qualified open-model access and continuing advanced-agent risk research.

*   **Steve Wozniak**, Apple cofounder. [Signed](https://futureoflife.org/fli_pause-giant-ai-experiments_an-open-letter/) the call to pause training systems more powerful than GPT-4 until safety could catch up.

He also [posted on X](https://x.com/mustafasuleyman/status/2099488602418028917?s=20), "AI must be subordinate and always in service of people." Microsoft AI also recently published a [Code of Conduct](https://microsoft.ai/code-of-conduct/) asserting that AI "is not conscious and should not be designed to imitate consciousness," while acknowledging in the same document, "the science of AI consciousness is far from settled."

In a Dwarkesh Patel [interview](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/carl-shulman-2), he estimated a one-in-four to one-in-five chance of an AI takeover, discussing possible pathways like cyberattacks, biological weapons or gaining access to military infrastructure.

*   **Joe Carlsmith**, Anthropic researcher who works on Claude's values and constitution, former senior advisor at Open Philanthropy, now Coefficient Giving. In a 2021 [report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13353), he developed a six-step argument for how misaligned, power-seeking AI could permanently disempower humanity, initially estimating a roughly 5% chance of catastrophe by 2070, then in 2022, he updated his estimate to greater than 10%.

*   **Katja Grace**, cofounder and lead researcher at AI Impacts. In a series of 2026 [essays](https://worldspiritsockpuppet.com/) on AI risk, including _Why are people so scared of causing fear?_, she wrote in another essay, "I think of AI unemployment and AI extinction risk as basically the same issue, and in likely scenarios, happening together."

*   **Michael K. Cohen and Maike Osborne** (then publishing as Michael A. Osborne), who were both Oxford researchers at the time, submitted 2022 [evidence](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/113797/html/) to the UK Parliament warning that "Certain forms of advanced AI would present an existential risk to us."

*   **Melanie Mitchell**, Santa Fe Institute professor and AI and cognitive science researcher. In a 2023 _The Hub_[interview](https://thehub.ca/podcast/audio/is-ai-an-existential-threat-yann-lecun-max-tegmark-melanie-mitchell-and-yoshua-bengio-make-their-case/), she shared skepticism that AI poses an existential threat, saying "Our society, our institutions, our technologies give us these layers of complexity that protect us in some way against any sudden shock. *   **Paul Christiano**, OpenAI Foundation board member and Safety and Security Committee member and senior tech advisor at NIST's Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI). In September 2026 he [announced](https://paulfchristiano.substack.com/p/personal-statement-on-joining-the) on his substack that he's joining OpenAI nonprofit board, serving on the Safety and Security Committee to support safety oversight, writing, "Based on the recent trajectory of capabilities and the continued difficulty of alignment, I now believe there is a meaningful risk that rapid acceleration in AI capabilities leads to catastrophic and irreversible loss of control in the very near term."

*   **Sara Hooker**, former VP of Research at Cohere, former Google Brain researcher and co-founder and CEO of Adaption. *   **Andrew Critch and David Krueger**, AI safety researchers. In their 2020 [report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04948)_AI Research Considerations for Human Existential Safety (ARCHES)_, they analyze existential risks from advanced AI and technical research directions that could reduce them.

*   **Richard Ngo**, former alignment researcher at DeepMind and governance researcher at OpenAI, now independent. *   **Roman Yampolskiy**, University of Louisville professor and AI-safety researcher. Long-time advocate for preventing the development of superintelligence citing the catastrophic risk. In a 2020 [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04071) on arXiv, he warns, "There seems to be no evidence to conclude that a less intelligent agent can indefinitely maintain control over a more intelligent agent."

*   **Margaret Boden** (1936–2025), University of Sussex research professor and pioneer in artificial intelligence and cognitive science. *   **François Chollet**, Keras creator and cofounder of the ARC Prize Foundation. In a 2017 [essay](https://medium.com/%40francois.chollet/the-impossibility-of-intelligence-explosion-5be4a9eda6ec), he rejected the idea of a runaway intelligence explosion and [posted on X](https://x.com/fchollet/status/2099230720753598471?s=20) in 2026, "One of the most worrying risks linked to frontier AI is extreme power concentration. *   **Luciano Floridi**, Founding Director of Yale's Digital Ethics Center. *   **Toby Walsh**, chief scientist at the University of New South Wales Artificial Intelligence Institute. *   **Stephen Hawking**(1942–2018), Cambridge theoretical physicist and cosmologist. In a 2014 BBC [interview](https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-30290540) he warned that "The development of full artificial intelligence could spell the end of the human race."

*   **Ben Goertzel**, SingularityNET CEO, OpenCog founder and longtime artificial general intelligence (AGI) researcher.
